## Design Summary

The canonical complaint and false-arrest contracts already require
claim–Defendant–challenged-act mappings, relevant-time knowledge,
fact-to-element application, causation, injury, and Defendant-specific
qualified-immunity analysis. They do not expressly reject a common shortcut:
actor headings followed by omnibus incorporation, a paragraph range, and a
conclusion that leaves the court to construct the application. The approved
correction defines one functionally closed actor unit and adds a paired
synthetic evaluation fixture.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative A: Create another qualified-immunity skill

- **Approach:** Move the closed-unit requirement into a new specialized skill.
- **Advantages:** Gives the topic a dedicated package.
- **Disadvantages:** Duplicates standards already owned by the canonical
  complaint contract and creates another composition boundary.
- **Why not selected:** The failure is enforcement, not missing doctrine or a
  missing skill.

### Alternative B: Expand the deterministic complaint checker

- **Approach:** Parse complaint prose and mechanically decide whether every
  actor unit performs a sufficient factual and legal application.
- **Advantages:** Produces an apparent automatic gate.
- **Disadvantages:** Semantic closure, legal application, causation, and QI fit
  are judgment questions outside the checker's structural boundary.
- **Why not selected:** It would falsely represent legal-sufficiency judgment as
  deterministic validation.

### Alternative C: Positive contract plus paired semantic fixture

- **Approach:** Define the required closed actor-unit shape in the canonical and
  false-arrest references, make completion audit fail on the known shortcuts,
  and preserve paired failing/passing candidates with independent rubric
  criteria.
- **Advantages:** Strengthens the existing owners, directly targets the observed
  behavior, and retains the mechanical checker boundary.
- **Disadvantages:** Deterministic fixture checks can identify an explicit
  shortcut but cannot alone prove semantic closure.

## Agreed Approach

Use Alternative C. Functional closure, not a fixed paragraph count, controls.
The complaint must state its own decisive bridge; a supporting brief may expand
but cannot cure it. In an arrest-time actor unit, later facts may serve later
functions only when the unit expressly excludes them from the earlier knowledge
set. The fixture will use deterministic grading only for a narrow explicit
shortcut and will reserve substantive discrimination to independent rubric
judgment.
