# Response to a motion for summary judgment

The most localized and most evidence-bound document in the case. Run the
localization protocol before writing a word: the required format of the fact
statement varies by district and failing it can deem the movant's facts
admitted.

## Deadline

Set by local rule or the scheduling order. Check the cache.

## Standard of review (the law's voice)

Summary judgment is proper only when there is no genuine dispute as to any
material fact. Fed. R. Civ. P. 56(a). The evidence is viewed and inferences
drawn in the light most favorable to the nonmovant. Recite with citations to the
forum's binding authority.

## The three parts

1. The response to the movant's statement of facts, in the district's exact
   required format: paragraph-by-paragraph admit or dispute, with a record
   citation for every dispute. Rule 56(c)(1). An unsupported "disputed" is an
   admission.
2. A statement of additional material facts, if the district provides for one,
   carrying the plaintiff's version with record citations.
3. The memorandum: introduction, standard, argument mapping the disputed facts
   onto each element the motion attacks, conclusion.

## Evidence rules

- Use `drafting-section-1983-declarations-and-evidence` to classify proposed
  declaration statements, prepare the unsigned factual declaration and
  exhibit-foundation map, and preserve human declarant approval and execution
  boundaries.
- Pleadings are not evidence at this stage. Every disputed fact needs an
  affidavit, a declaration under 28 U.S.C. § 1746, deposition testimony, or an
  exhibit. The plaintiff's own sworn declaration of what they saw and
  experienced is competent evidence; write it in the writing system, first
  person, facts only.
- Cite the record precisely: exhibit, page, paragraph or line. Rule 56(c)(1)(A).
- If discovery needed to oppose the motion has not happened, file a Rule 56(d)
  declaration stating the specific facts sought and why they are unavailable,
  and ask for time; do not oppose on an empty record.

## Qualified immunity

Answer both prongs with record citations: evidence from which a jury could find
the violation, and the authority that clearly established the right at the time.
Element recitations track the controlling authority's wording per the writing
system.

## Discipline rules

- The fact statement wins or loses the motion; the memorandum explains it. Spend
  the effort in that order.
- Dispute only what the record lets you dispute. A page of manufactured disputes
  buries the three genuine ones.
