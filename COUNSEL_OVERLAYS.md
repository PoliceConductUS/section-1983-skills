# Defense-Counsel Litigation Profiles

Defense-counsel overlays are evidence-coded professional litigation profiles.
They describe verified identity, time-bounded counsel teams, sourced litigation
behavior, courts' treatment of that behavior, and calibrated professional next-
move forecasts. They do not profile personalities or private lives.

[Manage case overlays](OVERLAYS.md) owns the shared inventory, precedence,
filing-manifest, immutable-version, and supersession rules. This guide owns
counsel-specific research, attribution, calibration, and lifecycle triggers.

## Professional scope

Study only public professional litigation behavior: recurring defenses and
boilerplate; qualified immunity, standing, mootness, waiver, pleading, and
procedural-default arguments; treatment of factual disputes and video at Rule
12; repeated authorities; alternative arguments; responses to narrowed claims;
and public amendment, discovery, sanctions, scheduling, and settlement-position
patterns.

Exclude family, politics, private life, protected traits, rumors, personality
assessments, irrelevant social media, and other personal material. Do not infer
strategy from personality. Do not threaten, deceive, harass, or recommend
manipulating an adversary or judge. Do not make a case-outcome prediction or a
judicial behavior prediction.

Keep three profiles separate: the Judicial Reasoning Profile describes the
assigned judge's verified decisional method; the defense-counsel profile
describes supported professional litigation behavior; controlling law states
what governing authority permits. None supplies facts or conclusions for
another.

## Source hierarchy and research record

Use approved public sources in this order:

1. the current docket and filed papers;
2. official court dockets, opinions, orders, hearing transcripts, and oral-
   argument records;
3. CourtListener and RECAP public records;
4. official bar directories for identity and status;
5. firm biographies for identity, affiliation, and expressly claimed experience
   only;
6. attorney-authored public articles and CLE materials as bounded professional
   context only; and
7. other approved public case artifacts with verified identity.

Only filed papers and official proceeding records establish litigation behavior.
A bar entry, biography, article, CLE presentation, or social-media post does not
establish that counsel made an argument in a case.

Record exact queries, systems searched, search and retrieval dates, checked-
through date, scope, deduplication method, candidate denominator, retrieved
records, unresolved records, unavailable records, source IDs, and fingerprints.
Do not invoke paid PACER or another fee-incurring source without separate
explicit authorization. Record inaccessible or fee-gated documents as scoped
missingness, not as absent behavior.

## Identity, attribution, and counsel teams

Maintain reusable identity separately from event-driven behavior. Verify each
attorney's professional name, bar status and checked date, firm affiliation,
appearance, represented party, and effective dates from the source appropriate
to that fact.

Distinguish these roles:

- **signer**: the filed paper or signature record identifies the signer;
- **named author**: the source expressly identifies the author;
- **oral advocate**: an official proceeding record identifies the speaker;
- **appearance counsel**: the docket establishes an appearance;
- **listed counsel**: the person appears in a caption or signature block; and
- **counsel team**: the sourced group responsible for a filing or position when
  individual authorship is not established.

Appearance counsel and listed counsel are not automatically authors. A jointly
filed paper defaults to counsel team behavior. Assign it to an individual only
when a direct approved source supports signer, named author, oral advocate, or
equivalent authorship.

Build each counsel team for one matter and effective date range. Link it to the
represented parties and applicable litigation-alignment groups. A firm name,
caption, or joint appearance does not create permanent alignment.

## Historical arguments and judicial treatment

Historical counsel arguments preserve the case, court, docket, posture,
represented party and group, exact filing/page/heading/quotation, claim,
challenged act, element or defense, qualified-immunity prong when applicable,
requested relief, attribution role, date, and sources.

Judicial treatment remains a separate court-authored record linked to the exact
argument. A court's recommendation, adoption, rejection, modification,
supersession, reversal, vacatur, or unresolved treatment is not counsel conduct.
Track court-documented loss patterns only from those linked treatment records.

Keep four canonical layers separate:

1. historical counsel arguments;
2. judicial treatment of those arguments;
3. current-case attacks linked from the litigation-alignment overlay; and
4. forecasted next moves.

Compare different cross-case positions by exact source and posture. A difference
is not automatically waiver, concession, judicial estoppel, misconduct, or bad
faith. Any claimed legal effect requires a separate verified legal analysis.

## Patterns and calibrated forecasts

The useful output is a bounded forecast of a professional litigation move, not
the label that an attorney "likes" an argument. For every pattern or forecast,
declare the comparable corpus, scope, selection method, denominator, coded
count, unresolved and unavailable missingness, posture, supporting examples,
contrary examples, source IDs, checked-through date, limits, and calibrated
confidence.

An incomplete denominator supports only bounded examples and gaps. It does not
support `often`, `usually`, a recurring-pattern claim, a loss rate, or a
forecast. A complete record may support low, moderate, or high confidence that a
team **may** make a defined next move. Never say counsel will, always, or never
act. Never convert `often` into `will`, predict the result, or predict how the
judge will respond.

Court-documented rejections, criticized authority readings, refused factual
characterizations, failed motions, and appellate reversals remain separately
attributed treatment evidence. Preserve adopted or successful contrary examples
as well as losses.

## Review and filing composition

Run the blind common-attack review without any counsel overlay. The actual-
adversary review receives only the validated counsel team and professional
history relevant to the target litigation-alignment group, claim, defendants,
challenged acts, posture, and effective date.

A forecast remains labeled advisory context. It is not an actual attack and
cannot suppress a common attack. The independent blind review still tests the
complete common-attack checklist.

The filing-version manifest pins the Judicial Reasoning Profile, litigation-
alignment overlay, individual-attorney identity overlay, and counsel-team
behavior overlay separately. Apply controlling law independently. A stale,
irrelevant, mismatched, or failing overlay produces no specialized drafting
change. Pass that manifest to the counsel validator so both counsel pins are
checked against the exact research snapshot and overlay fingerprint.

## Create, reuse, refresh, rebuild, and supersede

- **Create** an identity overlay after identity and status are verified. Create
  a team overlay after an approved appearance, filing, or proceeding establishes
  the team. Create a behavior pattern or forecast only after the declared public
  corpus supports its evidence strength.
- **Reuse** only when identity/status, team membership, representation,
  alignment scope, source snapshot, checked-through date, corpus coverage, and
  validator result remain current.
- **Refresh** into a new immutable version when checked-through research
  advances without a material conclusion change.
- **Rebuild** after appearance, withdrawal, substitution, changed alignment, a
  new signed filing or oral argument, verified identity/status change, new court
  treatment, material public evidence, corrected attribution, or user scope
  change.
- **Supersede** without editing, overwriting, moving, renaming, or deleting the
  prior version. Record the invalidation event and replacement.

A user override may add or exclude research or review scope. It cannot rewrite
source provenance, attribution, checked dates, historical records, forecasts, or
prior immutable versions.

## Synthetic substitution and realignment lifecycle

1. Attorneys Alpha and Beta enter appearances for one fictional officer group.
   Create separate identity records and one time-bounded team. Their appearance
   establishes no individual authorship.
2. A joint motion identifies Alpha as signer but no named author. Attribute the
   motion to the counsel team; record Alpha's signer role without assigning
   authorship to Beta.
3. Approved public records supply three comparable pleading-stage matters, two
   supporting examples, one contrary example, and two court-treatment records. A
   validated moderate-confidence next-move forecast may follow.
4. Beta withdraws and Attorney Gamma appears for a different litigation-
   alignment group. Preserve the first team and forecast, create a new immutable
   team version, and rebuild only the slices affected by the substitution and
   realignment.
5. A later public order treats one historical argument. Add a separate judicial-
   treatment record and reevaluate the pattern without rewriting what counsel
   argued.
