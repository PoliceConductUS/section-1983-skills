# Amended complaint and motion for leave to amend

## First question: is leave even needed?

Rule 15(a)(1): one amendment as of right within 21 days after serving
the complaint, or within 21 days after service of the answer or a Rule
12(b) motion, whichever is earlier. Inside that window, file the amended
complaint; no motion.

## When leave is required

Rule 15(a)(2): the court should freely give leave when justice so
requires. The recognized reasons to deny: undue delay, bad faith,
repeated failure to cure, undue prejudice to the opponent, and futility.
_Foman v. Davis_, 371 U.S. 178 (1962). The motion addresses each.

## Motion skeleton

1. Caption and title
2. What the amendment does, in one short paragraph (adds a defendant,
   adds facts learned in discovery, cures a pleading defect the court or
   an R&R identified)
3. Why leave should be granted: timing, diligence, no prejudice, not
   futile, mapped to the _Foman_ reasons that could be raised
4. Attachments: the complete proposed amended complaint, and a redline
   against the operative complaint if the localization cache requires
   one
5. Conclusion and proposed order if the district requires one

## The amended complaint itself

- It supersedes the original entirely. Replead everything, not just the
  changes; a fact left out is a fact abandoned.
- It follows `documents/complaint.md` and the writing system in full.
- Title it by number ("First Amended Complaint") and keep paragraph
  numbering stable where possible so prior record citations survive.

## Discipline rules

- Amend once, well, rather than often. Repeated amendment is itself a
  reason for denial.
- When responding to a dismissal motion, ask for leave in the
  alternative and say what the amendment would add; a bare request
  without the proposed pleading is routinely denied.
