# False-arrest complaint delta

This reference adds false-arrest-specific requirements only after the canonical
general complaint contract is available. It does not supply a fallback
whole-document skeleton, complete count-field list, general qualified-immunity
matrix, or general Monell contract.

## Decision-centered chronology and seizure boundary

State each implicated stage in its actual chronological order:

1. **Pre-seizure setting:** location, lawful activity, observable conduct, and
   each officer's arrival.
2. **Investigation:** questions, answers, commands, compliance, observations,
   records checks, warnings, and stated offense.
3. **Seizure boundary:** show of authority or physical restraint, submission,
   exact time or narrowest supported interval, decisionmaker, and joining
   actors.
4. **Arrest-time basis:** facts each participant knew before or at that
   boundary.
5. **Later stages:** physical contact, alleged resistance, force, handcuffing,
   transport, booking, custody, reports, prosecution, and disposition, each
   included only when material and kept separate by actor and time.
6. **Injury:** the injury caused by each challenged act and defendant.

Do not let a later fact migrate backward into the arrest-time knowledge set. Use
a separate subsection or paragraph group for each implicated stage. Each
numbered factual paragraph covers one material event or a closely connected set
of circumstances within one stage. Name the actor, state what that actor
observed or knew then, and state what that actor said or did. When a time,
speaker, wording, or sequence is unknown, plead the supported interval or
attribution and record the remainder as a GAP. Do not supply precision by
inference.

| Stage                                           | Include                                                                                                                                                                                            | Do not include, do, or say                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ----------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Pre-seizure setting                             | Date or supported interval, location, plaintiff's activity, conduct observable to each officer, and each officer's arrival                                                                         | Unobserved motive, irrelevant personal history, later-acquired knowledge, or an adjective standing in for observable conduct                                                                                      |
| Investigation                                   | Speaker and recipient of each material question, command, answer, warning, observation, or completed records check; exact words when supported and legally material                                | Passive wording that hides the speaker; "refused," "failed to comply," or "interfered" without the command, response, timing, and observable conduct supporting the characterization                              |
| Arrest decision                                 | Decisionmaker, announcement or communication, suspected offense, time, facts known to each participant, transmission of facts between officers, and facts bearing on each material offense element | Later resistance, force, reports, charging decisions, or disposition; collective knowledge without facts showing communication; "no probable cause" as a substitute for the offense-element factual chain         |
| Seizure boundary                                | Show of authority or physical restraint, submission, earliest supported time or interval, first restraining actor, and when each later officer joined                                              | Later handcuffing or touching as the seizure start when earlier submission is supported; an exact second the sources do not establish                                                                             |
| Physical contact, alleged resistance, and force | Sequence, actor, restraint status, attributed allegation of resistance, force type, duration, warnings, observable threat or flight, and resulting injury                                          | Alleged resistance as admitted fact; post-seizure conduct as pre-seizure knowledge; distinct force acts in one collective allegation; "the video clearly shows" when the view or audio is incomplete or ambiguous |
| Continued custody                               | A later decision, responsible actor, then-known facts, duration, causal mechanism, and injury when a distinct detention theory or damages allegation makes the stage material                      | Routine transport, booking, or jail detail that supports no claim, inference, duration, or injury; a later detention theory merged into the original arrest claim                                                 |
| Reports, prosecution, and disposition           | Author, date, material statement or omission, transmission or use, charge, supported procedural steps, exact disposition, and a distinct later injury when material                                | A later account as arrest-time knowledge; "false," "fabricated," or "malicious" without supporting facts; a statement that dismissal, suppression, or nonprosecution itself proves lack of probable cause         |
| Injury and damages                              | The act, responsible defendant, injury, onset, duration, treatment or expense when supported, and requested category of relief                                                                     | An omnibus injury list unconnected to an act or defendant; unsupported severity, permanence, diagnosis, expense, or causation                                                                                     |

Keep the facts section factual. Put the offense-element label, probable-cause
application, and extended authority analysis in the place required by the
canonical general contract or in internal work product.

## Arrest-time offense matrix

Complete one row for every stated or charged offense and every alternative
offense actually raised by the defense, a controlling ruling, or governing law.
Do not add every conceivable offense.

| Offense | Required element | Arrest-time supporting fact | Contrary or absent fact | Defendant who knew it | Source or GAP |
| ------- | ---------------- | --------------------------- | ----------------------- | --------------------- | ------------- |

State each offense's required elements from the statute and pin them to the
controlling state-court construction that fixes those elements. Do not
paraphrase an offense from memory or state only the statute number. Record the
statutory citation and construction authority, or a GAP, in the source column.

For each alternative offense actually raised by the defense, a controlling
ruling, or governing law, identify each incorporated-record fact or conduct that
remains unresolved or ambiguous and is material to an offense element. Without
admitting that fact or conduct occurred, map the unresolved fact to the offense
element and either state the supported element-level reason it does not supply
probable cause or arguable probable cause or record a filing-critical GAP for
reserved strategy decision. Do not inventory merely conceivable offenses.

Apply the specialization in this order:

> **Offense → element → decisive facts → contemporaneous knowledge →
> probable-cause application.**

Use concrete negative facts only when supported and material to an offense
element, such as no threat, obstruction, force, flight, warning, question,
described impairment, or refusal. Do not copy a generic list of negatives into
the complaint. An assertion that an element was absent does not replace the
facts showing its absence.

Route the false-arrest theory before drafting:

- **Warrantless arrest:** identify the seizure point, each officer's
  contemporaneous knowledge, and the offense-element map.
- **Warrant-based arrest:** identify the contributor and transmission chain,
  challenged statement or omission, corrected affidavit, materiality, and any
  applicable intermediary doctrine.
- **Post-process detention:** identify the separate decision, defendant,
  knowledge set, causal chain, and injury. Do not merge it with the original
  arrest.

## Actor matrix

| Defendant | Arresting-officer status | Caller-declared primary? | Knowledge at relevant time | Personal act | Event stage | Causal role | Particular injury | GAP |
| --------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------ | -------------------------- | ------------ | ----------- | ----------- | ----------------- | --- |

Before completing the actor matrix, audit the declared inputs to determine
whether an arrest occurred. For each source-documented arresting officer, record
the arresting-officer status and whether the caller declared that officer
primary. One identified arresting officer is primary. If more than one arresting
officer is identified without a caller-declared primary, stop and ask for the
designation; never infer it from rank, chronology, report authorship, physical
contact, or a prior filing's order.

Do not assign every arrest theory to every officer. Use "Defendants" as a
collective subject only when every named defendant performed the same alleged
act or received the same communicated fact. Presence, employment, partnership,
or arrival after the initial seizure does not establish participation. A
later-arriving officer requires a separate supported theory for continued
seizure, force, intervention, report conduct, or later custody.

## False-arrest application additions

Within each applicable mapping required by the canonical general contract,
identify the suspected offense, disputed offense element, seizure point,
arrest-time knowledge, personal participation, and probable-cause application
for that defendant. Address an alternative offense or arguable probable cause
only when the defense, a controlling ruling, or governing law makes it material.
Do not rely only on a general right to be free from arrest.

Keep initial arrest, later force, continued detention, report conduct, and
prosecution separate when their actors, decisions, knowledge sets, causal paths,
or injuries differ. Incorporate only the factual paragraphs that perform work
for the false-arrest theory.

When the canonical fair-warning analysis applies to a false-arrest count, frame
the right at the level of the suspected offense, disputed element, seizure
stage, and facts known to that defendant. This is an application of the
canonical general requirement, not a separate qualified-immunity contract.

## Warrants and challenged accounts

When a warrant, affidavit, report, or later-added justification matters, state:

1. the challenged statement or omission and its timing;
2. the concrete contradiction and source;
3. the corrected account;
4. materiality to the decision the account could affect; and
5. each defendant's transmission, authorship, approval, or causal contribution.

A post-arrest report cannot supply facts known at the earlier arrest. It may be
relevant to continued custody, prosecution, review, or another later injury only
if the elements and causal chain support that theory.

## Video and incorporated-material rule

- Describe only what a recording continuously and unambiguously resolves.
- Identify occlusion, missing audio, uncertain speaker attribution, competing
  inferences, and timing limits.
- Preserve exact event timing. Never move the seizure later to fit a cleaner
  timestamp.
- Audit the complete recording or document, not only a favorable excerpt.
  Identify each contradiction, ambiguity, additional offense theory, later-only
  fact, or defense-favorable characterization before deciding whether to
  incorporate or attach it.
- Incorporate or attach a recording, report, affidavit, or disposition document
  only when the pleading benefit exceeds the risk that the complete material
  controls over or narrows the allegations.
- Do not attach material merely because one excerpt is favorable or because it
  may be useful in discovery or at summary judgment.
- A dismissal, suppression ruling, or nonprosecution outcome does not by itself
  establish absence of probable cause.

## False-arrest compression and amendment audit

For each paragraph, classify it:

| Keep                                                                                                | Move to internal work product                                                         | Combine                                                            | Omit                                                                        |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Decisive false-arrest fact, necessary context, supported application or inference, or needed relief | Source methodology, extended authority discussion, discovery plan, evidentiary detail | Repeated chronology, duplicated standards, overlapping allegations | Unsupported label, immaterial history, redundant rhetoric, abandoned theory |

Then ask:

- Can the court locate the seizure boundary immediately?
- Does each factual paragraph stay within one event stage and identify the
  actor, then-known facts, and act?
- Is every arrest-time fact separated from later conduct?
- Does each command, refusal, resistance, interference, or similar
  characterization state the speaker, words or conduct, timing, and
  source-supported basis?
- Does each offense-element conclusion rest on pleaded facts?
- Does every named defendant have a personal act and causal role?
- Does each later custody, report, prosecution, or disposition paragraph support
  a distinct claim, inference, duration, injury, or requested relief? If not,
  omit it.
- Does any collective actor, motive, credibility, or liability label substitute
  for supported facts?
- Has every incorporated recording or document been audited in full, including
  defense-favorable portions and alternative-offense implications?
- Can any arrest theory be removed without weakening a supported theory?
- Is the complaint functioning as a short pleading rather than a source memo or
  summary-judgment brief?

The target is not the corpus median. The target is the shortest false-arrest
pleading that preserves every fact necessary to answer the actual dismissal
grounds.
