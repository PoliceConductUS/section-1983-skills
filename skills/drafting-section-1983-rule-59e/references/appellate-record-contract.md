# Appellate-record contract

Read this reference when the Rule 59(e) filing should preserve a clean record
for review, when denial is expected, or when amendment may affect supersession
or appealability.

## Court-facing posture

The filing seeks district-court relief. Do not tell the district court that the
motion exists mainly to prepare an appeal. Make the record reviewable by stating
the issues, sources, preservation, and requested rulings directly.

## Cold-reader record

The motion and appendix should let a reader verify without reconstructing prior
briefing:

1. the challenged ruling and judgment;
2. the Rule 59(e) ground;
3. the amendment instruction or procedure;
4. the pre-judgment cure notice and request to lodge the complete pleading;
5. the implicit or express denial;
6. the complete proposed pleading;
7. the strongest examples showing nonfutility; and
8. the alternative rulings requested.

Reproduce in the appendix the record pages needed to verify load-bearing
quotations and preservation statements.

Append the specific pages of prior briefing that contain a load-bearing request
or preservation statement. Do not reproduce an entire earlier brief merely to
make the appendix longer. Include the full document only when a court rule,
incorporation issue, or context needed to understand the cited excerpt requires
it.

## Reviewable-error ledger

| Error | Challenged ruling | Governing standard | Preservation cite | Exhibit A cite | Requested ruling | Brief location |
| ----- | ----------------- | ------------------ | ----------------- | -------------- | ---------------- | -------------- |

Do not merge different legal functions. A row should state whether it supports
reopening under Rule 59(e), leave under Rule 15(a), nonfutility under Rule 12,
or more than one through separate explanations.

## Claim-preservation floor

The concise filed crosswalk must account for every claim for which relief is
sought. The full internal claim ledger must identify every claim, defendant,
dismissal ground, proposed cure, complaint paragraphs, authority status, and
requested ruling.

For a theory dismissed without a claim-specific analysis, identify that
procedural fact and request either leave or a ruling naming the deficient
element. Do not invent a defect to fill the court's silence.

## Supersession and abandonment audit

Before tendering an amended complaint, record:

- each claim and defendant in the operative complaint;
- each claim and defendant in Exhibit A;
- every omission, narrowing, or changed theory;
- whether the change is voluntary, court-directed, unsupported, or unresolved;
- the expected supersession effect; and
- the text needed to preserve review of any court-excluded claim.

When the court permits only a conformed subset, do not describe excluded claims
as voluntarily abandoned. Keep the requested ruling and preservation position
consistent across the brief, proposed order, and later notice of appeal.

## Alternative-ground audit

Assume the ruling may be affirmed on a ground apparent in the record even if the
district court did not state that ground. Audit and answer each plausible
amendment ground:

- delay and diligence;
- bad faith;
- repeated failure to cure;
- prejudice;
- futility;
- failure to supply a complete pleading or adequate cure notice;
- arguments or facts available before judgment; and
- any case-specific procedural bar.

State the record fact that answers each ground. Do not rely on the district
court's failure to mention it.

## Opposition and reply forecast

Before finalizing the opening brief, identify the strongest likely response to
each load-bearing point. Ensure the opening filing contains the facts and
authorities necessary to answer predictable anti-rehash, delay, withholding,
futility, waiver, and supersession arguments. A reply may answer the opposition;
it should not become the first place a necessary preservation fact appears.

## Outcome branches

Audit the consequences of:

1. denial of Rule 59(e);
2. full leave and later dismissal of Exhibit A;
3. partial leave with a court-directed conformed complaint;
4. partial survival that delays appeal; and
5. a claim-specific immunity or futility ruling.

The proposed order should create a clear branch: vacate and accept Exhibit A;
alternatively identify permitted claims and set a deadline for a conformed
pleading; or identify each denied claim, defendant, and deficient element or
immunity prong.

Keep the full consequences analysis internal. Put only the requested
district-court branches in the motion, brief, and proposed order. Use one
branched order with a claim-disposition schedule unless local practice requires
separate orders.

## Final appellate-record audit

Confirm:

- each issue was raised at the earliest required stage and again only as needed;
- the Rule 59 motion states the precise error and requested correction;
- every procedural quotation is verifiable in the appendix;
- Exhibit A and the crosswalk use stable paragraph references;
- no brief assertion supplies a missing complaint allegation;
- full and alternative relief are consistent across documents;
- the supersession inventory is complete;
- no necessary point first appears in a projected reply; and
- the filing does not claim a judge tendency unsupported by a current corpus.
