## Whole-complaint spine

Use this order unless the governing court or case requires a different one:

1. Caption, title, and jury demand.
2. A short outcome-first introduction when it helps orient the court.
3. Parties, capacities, and color of law.
4. Jurisdiction and venue.
5. Chronological facts using the stage-by-stage contract below.
6. Separate counts identifying the right, defendant set, conduct, relevant-time knowledge, application, and injury.
7. Damages, requested relief, and jury demand.

An introduction is optional. A clear event sequence and separate counts are not.

## Decision-centered factual sequence

State implicated stages in their actual chronological order:

1. **Pre-seizure setting:** location, lawful activity, observable conduct, and officer arrival.
2. **Investigation:** questions, answers, commands, compliance, observations, records checks, warnings, and stated offense.
3. **Seizure boundary:** show of authority or physical restraint, submission, exact time or narrowest supported interval, decisionmaker, and joining officers.
4. **Arrest-time basis:** facts each participant knew before or at that boundary.
5. **Later stages:** physical contact, alleged resistance, force, handcuffing, transport, booking, custody, reports, prosecution, and disposition—each included only when material and kept separate by actor and time.
6. **Injury:** connect each injury to the act and defendant alleged to have caused it.

Do not let a later fact migrate backward into the arrest-time knowledge set.

## Stage-by-stage facts contract

Use a separate subsection or paragraph group for each implicated stage. Each numbered paragraph must cover one material event or a closely connected set of circumstances within one stage. Name the actor, state what that actor observed or knew then, and state what that actor said or did. Do not combine actors merely because they were present together. When a time, speaker, wording, or sequence is unknown, plead the supported interval or attribution and record the remainder as a GAP; do not supply precision by inference.

| Stage                                           | Include                                                                                                                                                                                            | Do not include, do, or say                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Pre-seizure setting                             | Date or supported interval, location, plaintiff's activity, conduct observable to each officer, and each officer's arrival                                                                         | Unobserved motive, irrelevant personal history, later-acquired knowledge, or an adjective standing in for observable conduct                                                                                                           |
| Investigation                                   | Speaker and recipient of each material question, command, answer, warning, observation, or completed records check; exact words when supported and legally material                                | Passive wording that hides the speaker; “refused,” “failed to comply,” or “interfered” without the command, response, timing, and observable conduct that support the characterization                                                 |
| Arrest decision                                 | Decisionmaker, announcement or communication, suspected offense, time, facts known to each participant, transmission of facts between officers, and facts bearing on each material offense element | Later resistance, force, reports, charging decisions, or disposition; a collective knowledge assertion without facts showing communication; “no probable cause” as a substitute for the offense-element factual chain                  |
| Seizure boundary                                | Show of authority or physical restraint, submission, earliest supported time or interval, first restraining actor, and when each later officer joined                                              | A later handcuffing or touching as the seizure start when earlier submission is supported; an exact second the sources do not establish                                                                                                |
| Physical contact, alleged resistance, and force | Sequence, actor, plaintiff's restraint status, attributed allegation of resistance, force type, duration, warnings, observable threat or flight, and resulting injury                              | Alleged resistance as an admitted fact; post-seizure conduct as pre-seizure knowledge; several officers' distinct force acts in one collective allegation; “the video clearly shows” when the view or audio is incomplete or ambiguous |
| Continued custody                               | A later decision, responsible actor, then-known facts, duration, causal mechanism, and injury when a distinct detention theory or damages allegation makes the stage material                      | Routine transport, booking, or jail detail that supports no claim, inference, duration, or injury; a later detention theory merged into the original arrest count                                                                      |
| Reports, prosecution, and disposition           | Author, date, material statement or omission, transmission or use, charge, supported procedural steps, exact disposition, and a distinct later injury when material                                | A later account as arrest-time knowledge; “false,” “fabricated,” or “malicious” without supporting facts; a statement that dismissal, suppression, or nonprosecution itself proves lack of probable cause                              |
| Injury and damages                              | The act, responsible defendant, injury, onset, duration, treatment or expense when supported, and requested category of relief                                                                     | An omnibus injury list unconnected to an act or defendant; severity, permanence, diagnosis, expense, or causation not supported by facts                                                                                               |

Keep the facts section factual. Plead the concrete facts that support or negate an offense element, but put the element label, probable-cause inference, qualified-immunity analysis, and extended authority discussion in the applicable count or internal matrix.

## Arrest-time offense matrix

Complete one row for every stated or charged offense and every alternative offense actually raised by the defense, a controlling ruling, or governing law. Do not add every conceivable offense.

| Offense | Required element | Arrest-time supporting fact | Contrary or absent fact | Defendant who knew it | Source or GAP |
| ------- | ---------------- | --------------------------- | ----------------------- | --------------------- | ------------- |

Draft the resulting allegation through:

> **Offense → element → decisive facts → contemporaneous knowledge → probable-cause application.**

Use concrete negative facts only when supported and material to an offense element: no threat, no obstruction, no force, no flight, no warning, no question asked, no described impairment, or no refusal. Do not copy a generic list of negatives into the complaint. A legal conclusion that an element was absent does not replace the facts showing its absence.

Route the theory before drafting:

- **Warrantless arrest:** seizure point, each officer’s contemporaneous knowledge, and an offense-element map.
- **Warrant-based arrest:** contributor and transmission chain, challenged statement or omission, corrected affidavit, materiality, and any intermediary doctrine.
- **Post-process detention:** a separate decision, defendant, knowledge set, causal chain, and injury; do not merge it with the original arrest.

## Actor matrix

| Defendant | Knowledge at relevant time | Personal act | Event stage | Causal role | Particular injury | Gap |
| --------- | -------------------------- | ------------ | ----------- | ----------- | ----------------- | --- |

Do not assign every claim to every officer. Use “Defendants” as a collective subject only when every named defendant performed the same alleged act or received the same communicated fact. Presence, employment, partnership, or arrival after the initial seizure does not establish participation. A later-arriving officer requires a separate supported theory for continued seizure, force, intervention, report conduct, or later custody.

## Count contract

Each count must contain:

1. **Element:** verified authority and the elements actually disputed.
2. **Decisive facts:** participant-specific allegations and precise incorporated paragraphs.
3. **Relevant-time knowledge:** what each defendant knew when that defendant performed the challenged act.
4. **Application:** the element-specific legal conclusion produced by those facts and that knowledge.
5. **Clearly established law:** for each individual and claim, the event date, precisely framed right or rule, verified binding pre-event authority, material factual similarities and differences, and why that authority gave this defendant fair warning.
6. **Result:** the claim, defendant, and requested ruling.

The decisive-facts, relevant-time-knowledge, and application functions are mandatory; their exact wording and paragraph count are not. State the application directly when the connection is clear. Expressly identify the inference when the application depends on an unobserved state of mind, agreement, causal mechanism, municipal attribution, or another non-obvious bridge. Do not add "These facts support the reasonable inference" as an incantation when a direct application already does the work.

Address alternative probable cause and arguable probable cause where the defense or controlling law makes them material. Do not rely only on a general right to be free from arrest.

Each count must identify the constitutional source, defendant and capacity, challenged act, event stage, disputed element or rule, decisive facts, relevant-time knowledge, relevant incorporated paragraphs, resulting application, any necessary non-obvious inference, particular injury, and relief. Incorporate only the factual paragraphs that do work in that count.

Do not repeat the entire chronology, incorporate every preceding paragraph, or combine the initial arrest, later force, continued detention, report conduct, and prosecution merely because they arose from one encounter. Do not name an officer in a count based only on presence or employment. Do not use “unreasonable,” “malicious,” “retaliatory,” “conspired,” “ratified,” “knew or should have known,” or a similar label as a substitute for the conduct and facts supporting that inference.

### Clearly-established-law matrix

Complete this matrix for every individual-capacity claim, including claims arising after the initial arrest:

| Claim | Defendant and challenged conduct | Event date | Precise right or rule | Binding authority and status | Decision date | Materially similar facts | Material differences | Why fair warning existed | GAP |
| ----- | -------------------------------- | ---------- | --------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------- | ------------------------ | -------------------- | ------------------------ | --- |

Apply these rules:

- Define the right at the level of the defendant’s alleged conduct, not as a general freedom from arrest, retaliation, force, conspiracy, or detention.
- Use verified Supreme Court or controlling circuit authority decided before the event. Classify every proposition as a holding, alternative holding, implicit holding, dicta, silence or non-holding, appellate factual statement, or persuasive-only authority, and check the rule of orderliness.
- Use state-law authority to establish the offense’s elements and construction where appropriate. Do not assume that a state decision defining an offense also clearly establishes the federal Fourth Amendment rule for qualified-immunity purposes; state its distinct role.
- Explain the factual match and candidly address differences. A string citation or quotation does not perform the fair-warning analysis.
- When several cases allegedly combine to supply fair warning, state the rule contributed by each case and use the combination only if verified authority supports treating that precedential chain as making the unlawfulness beyond debate.
- Do not use a district-court decision, nonprecedential decision, or later-decided case as the source of clearly established law. Label any such case’s limited persuasive use.
- If relying on an obvious-case route rather than materially similar precedent, identify verified controlling authority recognizing that route in the relevant context and explain why the alleged conduct satisfies its threshold. Do not invoke “obvious” as a label.
- Keep the full comparison in the internal matrix. In the complaint, state a concise claim-and-defendant-specific fair-warning paragraph that identifies the controlling rule, the materially similar conduct, and why the defendant had notice. Expand it only as required to answer an identified dismissal premise; do not turn the complaint into an authority brief.
- This concise complaint-level paragraph is **mandatory and non-waivable** for every individual-capacity claim against a qualified-immunity-eligible defendant. A case-specific strategy, control, routing, or "clearly-established goes in the brief only" instruction routes only the surplus analysis (full case stack, later history, competing authority, extended futility) to the brief and internal matrix; it never removes the paragraph from the complaint. A brief may add authority discussion; it never substitutes for it. Rule 8 and _Johnson v. City of Shelby_ do not lower this floor, because a plaintiff confronting qualified immunity in this circuit must plead facts defeating immunity with corresponding specificity; a prior qualified-immunity ruling or futility inquiry raises the required specificity. Only a defendant who cannot assert qualified immunity (e.g., a municipality on a Monell count) is exempt.
- If no supportable pre-event fair-warning path exists, record a filing-critical GAP and present the claim for strategy decision. Do not characterize the claim as filing-ready, silently omit it, or conceal the absence with a broad proposition.

### Warrants and challenged accounts

When a warrant, affidavit, report, or later-added justification matters, state:

1. challenged statement or omission and its timing;
2. concrete contradiction and source;
3. corrected account;
4. materiality to the decision the account could actually affect; and
5. each defendant’s transmission, authorship, approval, or causal contribution.

A post-arrest report cannot supply facts known at the earlier arrest. It may be relevant to continued custody, prosecution, review, or another later injury if the elements and causal chain support that theory.

## Video and incorporated-material rule

- Describe only what the recording continuously and unambiguously resolves.
- Identify occlusion, missing audio, uncertain speaker attribution, competing inferences, and timing limits.
- Preserve exact event timing; never move the seizure later to fit a cleaner timestamp.
- Audit the complete recording or document, not only the favorable excerpt. Identify any contradiction, ambiguity, additional offense theory, later-only fact, or defense-favorable characterization before deciding whether to incorporate or attach it.
- Incorporate or attach a recording, report, or disposition document only when the pleading benefit exceeds the risk that the complete material controls over or narrows the allegations. Do not attach material merely because one excerpt is favorable or because it may be useful in discovery or at summary judgment.
- A dismissal, suppression ruling, or nonprosecution outcome does not by itself establish absence of probable cause.

## Monell rule

For each municipal theory separately require:

1. identified municipal path;
2. concrete supporting facts;
3. reasonable municipal inference;
4. policymaker attribution and required notice;
5. particular injury; and
6. moving-force mechanism.

Statistics require a verified unit of count, comparable incidents, context, municipal exposure or notice, and a theory-specific causal connection. Discovery-controlled details may be pleaded on information and belief only when known facts already support the inference and the complaint identifies the information and its custodian.

Do not list every Monell path and say discovery will determine which one applies. Do not use later review, prosecution, civil defense, or records conduct as the moving force behind an earlier arrest unless supported facts establish a pre-arrest municipal act or omission and the required causal mechanism. Omit any municipal theory missing one of the six required steps and record the missing support as a GAP.

## Compression and amendment audit

For each paragraph, classify it:

| Keep                                                                                     | Move to internal work product                                                         | Combine                                                            | Omit                                                                        |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Decisive fact, necessary context, supported application or inference, element, or relief | source methodology, extended authority discussion, discovery plan, evidentiary detail | repeated chronology, duplicated standards, overlapping allegations | unsupported label, immaterial history, redundant rhetoric, abandoned theory |

Then ask:

- Can the court locate the seizure boundary immediately?
- Does each factual paragraph stay within one event stage and identify the actor, then-known facts, and act?
- Is every arrest-time fact separated from later conduct?
- Does each command, refusal, resistance, interference, or similar characterization state the speaker, words or conduct, timing, and source-supported basis?
- Does each offense-element conclusion rest on pleaded facts?
- Does every named defendant have a personal act and causal role?
- Does each later custody, report, prosecution, or disposition paragraph support a distinct claim, inference, duration, injury, or requested relief? If not, omit it.
- Does every count add a distinct right, defendant set, event, or injury?
- Does any collective actor, motive, credibility, or liability label substitute for supported facts?
- Has every incorporated recording or document been audited in full, including defense-favorable portions and alternative-offense implications?
- Can any claim be removed without weakening a supported theory?
- Is the complaint functioning as a short pleading rather than a source memo or summary-judgment brief?

The target is not the corpus median. The target is the shortest complaint that preserves every fact necessary to answer the actual dismissal grounds.
