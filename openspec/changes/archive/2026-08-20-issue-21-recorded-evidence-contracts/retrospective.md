# Retrospective

## What worked

- Treating three Markdown contracts as one public seam made the accidental
  checkout drift visible before the prose moved.
- Checking affirmative grammar and concrete inverted mutations prevented token
  presence from masquerading as semantic coverage.
- Preserving the dirty `main` files until branch/origin parity kept the prose
  recoverable throughout the transfer.

## What changed during implementation

The original Rule 59(e) list item combined event/video and statement/transcript
requirements under one `and`/`or` clause. Independent review showed that a
transcript-backed statement could appear to require visual confirmation. With
user approval, the final checkpoint now gives each evidence type its own primary
route and points both to the full express present-recollection route above.

The initial tests matched nearby tokens. Mutation review tightened them to
require affirmative obligations and incomplete-route failure conditions while
remaining bounded to the approved public contract.

## Future rule

When a recorded-evidence route changes, update complaint claim drafting,
complaint completion review, and Rule 59(e) packet review together. Add the
intended positive contract and at least one directly reversed mutation before
changing the prose. Keep event/video and statement/transcript routes separate.
