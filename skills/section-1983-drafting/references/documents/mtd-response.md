# Response to a motion to dismiss

## Deadline

Set by local rule, not the federal rules. Check the localization cache first.
Calendar it the day the motion arrives.

## Standard of review (the law's voice)

Recite with citations: the court accepts the complaint's factual allegations as
true and draws reasonable inferences for the plaintiff; the complaint must state
a claim that is plausible on its face. _Ashcroft v. Iqbal_, 556 U.S. 662 (2009);
_Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly_, 550 U.S. 544 (2007). A pro se complaint is
construed liberally. _Erickson v. Pardus_, 551 U.S. 89 (2007) (per curiam).

## Document skeleton

1. Caption and title responding to the motion by its docket number
2. Introduction: what the motion argues and why it fails, in three sentences or
   fewer
3. Standard of review
4. Argument, one section per ground the motion raises, in the motion's order: a.
   State the ground as the defendant framed it b. Answer it from the complaint's
   paragraphs, cited by number c. Answer the legal authority with the forum's
   binding authority
5. Alternative request: leave to amend rather than dismissal with prejudice, if
   any defect is curable (some districts require a separate motion; check the
   localization cache)
6. Conclusion stating the exact relief: deny the motion, or deny as to named
   counts

## Discipline rules

- Answer every ground. A ground met with silence is often treated as conceded.
- Sort the grounds: 12(b)(1) jurisdictional attacks get answered before 12(b)(6)
  merits attacks, because jurisdiction comes first.
- Stay inside the pleadings. Attaching new evidence invites conversion to
  summary judgment under Rule 12(d). Documents the complaint embraces (the
  incident report it quotes, the policy it describes) are the exception.
- Qualified immunity at the pleading stage: the question is whether the
  complaint's facts, taken as true, allege a violation of a clearly established
  right. Point at the paragraphs that do, then at the authority that clearly
  established it.
- The complaint is the record. Every factual assertion in the response cites a
  complaint paragraph. New facts belong in an amended complaint, not a brief.
