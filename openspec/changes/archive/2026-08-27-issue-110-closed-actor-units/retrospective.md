# Retrospective

## Outcome

Issue #110 closes an enforcement gap in the existing complaint and false-arrest
skills. It does not add a new qualified-immunity skill or a case-specific
pleading. The installed contracts now require each
claim–Defendant–challenged-act unit to contain its own compact factual bridge,
personal causation, and applicable qualified-immunity analysis.

## What worked

- Starting with a pressured behavioral baseline showed the actual failure mode:
  only two of five fresh-context outputs were closed actor units.
- Contract tests failed before implementation and passed after the reference
  changes.
- The paired fixture catches the explicit paragraph-range shortcut while its
  semantic rubric preserves the legal-review boundary.
- Five corrected fresh-context runs all produced closed actor units.
- Mutation checks established that the tests detect removal of the new
  paragraph-range and fixture rules.
- Independent review found and corrected the arguable-probable-cause scope and
  the distinction between general temporal exclusion and arrest-time limited
  use.

## Boundaries preserved

- No current case packet, complaint, motion, or control file was changed.
- The deterministic checker detects only the named textual shortcut; it does not
  certify legal sufficiency.
- The supporting brief may expand authority comparisons and record disputes, but
  it cannot supply a missing complaint-level application.
- Later-only facts are excluded from earlier knowledge generally. The additional
  duty to state an incorporated later fact's limited function remains specific
  to arrest-time units.

## Follow-through

Future complaint-skill changes should continue to pair narrow deterministic
checks with independent semantic pressure tests. Any broader enforcement of
filed-prose sufficiency would require a separately approved design rather than
expanding this fixture's mechanical rule.
