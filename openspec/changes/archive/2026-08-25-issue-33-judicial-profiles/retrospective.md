# Retrospective

## What worked

- Keeping profiles as validated data allowed one generic builder to replace an
  embedded real-person behavioral skill.
- The source-class and attribution model preserved what a source actually proves
  without averaging philosophy, self-presentation, outcomes, and independently
  revealed reasoning.
- Separate acquisition and compilation invocations made internet authority,
  approval, provenance, and later read-only reuse explicit.
- The common direct-root folder-package publisher supplied integrity and reload
  behavior without introducing CaseGraph or a repository-shaped output path.
- Synthetic temporary role contracts preserved receipt coverage without adding a
  public judge-specific dependency.

## Review correction

The first multi-operation folder contract listed `authorized` and `disabled` as
an allowed set. Fresh review found that the set did not bind either policy to an
operation, so a malformed host could cross the pairings. A new RED test required
failure before resolving nonexistent roots. The final contract maps
`acquisition` to `authorized` and `compilation` to `disabled`; each invocation
declares one known operation and its matching policy.

## Deferred by contract

- Issue #61 owns the shared fresh-process launcher that binds a validated

- Concrete judicial-review roles and profile variations remain downstream work;
  this builder does not launch agents, create role variants, or predict results.
