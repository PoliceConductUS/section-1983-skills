# Objection to a magistrate judge's report and recommendation

## Why this document matters

District courts routinely refer Section 1983 cases to magistrate judges. The
report and recommendation, called the R&R, is where cases get dismissed. The
objection is the plaintiff's one chance at fresh review by the district judge,
and a missed or general objection forfeits it.

## Deadline discipline (check this first)

- 14 days from service of the R&R to serve and file written objections. 28
  U.S.C. § 636(b)(1); Fed. R. Civ. P. 72.
- Add 3 days when the R&R was served by mail. Fed. R. Civ. P. 6(d).
- If the deadline is near, ask the court for an extension before it runs.

## Standard of review (state it, then meet it)

- Dispositive matters (dismissal, summary judgment, injunctions): the district
  judge reviews properly objected portions de novo. Fed. R. Civ. P. 72(b)(3); 28
  U.S.C. § 636(b)(1).
- Nondispositive matters (discovery, scheduling): the district judge sets aside
  a ruling only if it is clearly erroneous or contrary to law. Fed. R. Civ. P.
  72(a).
- Identify which kind of matter the R&R addresses and recite the matching
  standard, tracking the rule's wording with a citation (the law's voice).

## The specificity requirement

General objections get no review. "Plaintiff objects to the entire R&R" is
treated as no objection at all, and unobjected portions are reviewed, at most,
for clear error. Failure to object can waive appellate review. _Thomas v. Arn_,
474 U.S. 140 (1985). So:

- Number each objection.
- Tie each objection to a specific finding or conclusion, by page and section of
  the R&R.
- State what the R&R got wrong as a fact or a legal proposition, then show the
  correct result from the record and the controlling authority.

## Document skeleton

1. Caption, titled "Plaintiff's Objections to the Magistrate Judge's Report and
   Recommendation" with the R&R's docket number and date
2. Introduction (2–3 paragraphs: what the R&R recommends, that these objections
   are timely, and what relief the plaintiff seeks)
3. Standard of review
4. Objections, numbered, each with: a. The specific finding or conclusion
   objected to, with the R&R page b. Why it is wrong, mapped to the record and
   the law c. The result the district judge should reach instead
5. Conclusion (ask the court to reject or modify the identified portions and
   state the concrete outcome sought, such as denial of the motion to dismiss as
   to named counts)
6. Certificate of service

## Scope rules

- Argue from the record that was before the magistrate judge. Courts disfavor
  arguments and evidence raised for the first time in objections and may refuse
  to consider them.
- An objection is not a second brief on everything. Object to the findings that
  decide the requested result. Omit a harmless point without conceding it.
- State the R&R's adverse factual descriptions as the R&R's positions. Do not
  adopt them as facts without express user approval.
- The writing system applies with full force. An objection tempts a writer into
  intensifiers ("the R&R clearly erred") more than any other filing. State the
  error and prove it; delete the adverb.
