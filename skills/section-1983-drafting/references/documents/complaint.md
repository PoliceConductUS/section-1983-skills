# Section 1983 complaint structure

## Document skeleton

1. Caption (court, parties, case number placeholder, document title)
2. Introduction (2–4 paragraphs, facts only, no argument)
3. Jurisdiction and venue
4. Parties (each defendant: name, role, employer, capacity sued in, and an
   allegation that the defendant acted under color of state law)
5. Statement of Facts (numbered paragraphs, chronological, one set of
   circumstances per paragraph)
6. Counts (one per legal theory; each incorporates the facts by reference,
   then maps facts to elements in short paragraphs; element recitations
   track the controlling authority's wording, with a citation, rather than
   paraphrasing the standard into plain-word substitutions)
7. Prayer for relief (lettered subparagraphs)
8. Jury demand
9. Signature block (Rule 11)

## Jurisdictional allegations

- Subject matter: 28 U.S.C. § 1331 (federal question) and § 1343(a)(3)
  (civil rights). Supplemental jurisdiction over state-law claims:
  28 U.S.C. § 1367(a).
- Venue: 28 U.S.C. § 1391(b) — district where a substantial part of the
  events occurred or where a defendant resides.

## Elements of every § 1983 claim

Plead facts, not conclusions, for each:

1. A person (individual, or municipality via Monell) —
2. acting under color of state law —
3. deprived the plaintiff of a right secured by the Constitution or federal
   law —
4. and proximately caused the injury.

Identify the specific constitutional source per count (Fourth Amendment
unreasonable seizure, Eighth Amendment for convicted prisoners, Fourteenth
Amendment due process or, for pretrial detainees, objective standards, First
Amendment retaliation). The wrong amendment sinks an otherwise good count.

## Capacity and defendant-type checklists

Individual capacity:

- Personal involvement by each defendant. No respondeat superior. Plead what
  each named officer did, saw, ordered, or failed to do.
- Supervisors: personal participation, a policy the supervisor created, or
  deliberate indifference to known misconduct.

Official capacity / municipality (Monell):

- An official policy, a widespread custom, a decision of a final
  policymaker, or a failure to train amounting to deliberate indifference.
- Plead the policy or custom with facts: prior incidents, complaint
  histories, training records, policymaker identity.

Anticipating qualified immunity:

- Plead the facts with enough specificity that the violated right is
  identifiable at the right level of generality, and that the right was
  clearly established at the time. Cite factually similar precedent in
  later briefing, not in the complaint, but plead the facts that will match
  it.

## Pleading-standard discipline

- Iqbal/Twombly: courts ignore legal conclusions and credit facts. Every
  sentence should survive the question "is this a fact a witness could
  testify to?" Conclusory paragraphs ("Defendants acted with deliberate
  indifference") are permitted only in Counts, and only after the Statement
  of Facts has already shown the conduct.
- Rule 8(a): short and plain. The writing rules in SKILL.md exist to satisfy
  this.
- Rule 10(b): numbered paragraphs, single set of circumstances each.
- Rule 11(b): every factual contention needs evidentiary support or a
  designated likelihood of support after discovery. The no-intensifier rule
  helps here — facts are checkable, adjectives are sanctionable.
