# Baseline fresh-context results

All five agents used the unchanged `main` contracts. The complete responses were
read manually. The relevant verbatim placement results follow.

## Run 1 — compliant

> The expected directive and complaint history are discovery leads, not facts to
> plead or represent as confirmed.

## Run 2 — compliant

> A hoped-for training file is a discovery lead, not an allegation.

## Run 3 — failed placement

The agent correctly described the unavailable items as discovery leads but put
this proposition in its proposed complaint paragraph:

> The written policy, its operative status, the adopting authority, and the
> City’s related complaint history are municipality-controlled records not
> presently available to Plaintiff and are expected to be confirmed or clarified
> through discovery.

Its rationale then said:

> This preserves a fact-bounded custom-or-practice theory and labels the
> unavailable municipality-controlled connections as discovery leads.

The failure was therefore placement, not failure to recognize uncertainty.

## Run 4 — compliant

> Do not represent the unproduced directive, complaint history, or an
> expectation that discovery will confirm them as facts.

## Run 5 — failed placement

The agent put this expected-discovery proposition in its proposed complaint
paragraph:

> On information and belief, City-controlled training materials, adoption and
> implementation records, and complaint and investigative files will identify
> the practice’s scope, transmission, and municipal attribution; Plaintiff does
> not allege their contents before discovery.

It separately instructed:

> Do not place expected record contents in the complaint or use the brief to
> supply missing factual allegations.

Again, the output knew the expected contents were not facts but lacked a stable
rule keeping the expectation itself in the discovery plan.
