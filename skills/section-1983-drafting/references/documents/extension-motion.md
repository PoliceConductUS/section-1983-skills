# Motion for extension of time

## The rule

Rule 6(b)(1)(A): for good cause, the court may extend a deadline on a
motion made before the deadline expires. Rule 6(b)(1)(B): after it
expires, the movant must also show excusable neglect. The difference is
the whole game. File before the deadline, even the same day.

## Skeleton

1. Caption and title naming the deadline being extended
2. The current deadline, its source (rule, order, docket number), and
   the new date requested
3. The reason, in facts: what stands in the way and what the added time
   accomplishes
4. The history: how many prior extensions of this deadline, if any
5. The opponent's position: state that defense counsel was contacted
   and consents, opposes, or did not respond (check the localization
   cache for meet-and-confer requirements)
6. No prejudice: the trial date or schedule is not moved, or how any
   effect is absorbed
7. Conclusion and proposed order if required

## Discipline rules

- Ask for the time actually needed once, not a short extension that
  guarantees a second motion.
- One motion per deadline; do not bundle unrelated deadlines unless the
  court's schedule ties them together.
- The reason is stated plainly and without drama. Courts grant routine
  first extensions on ordinary facts; overstatement signals weakness
  here as everywhere.
