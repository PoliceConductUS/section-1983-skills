# Chapter 4: Bonus Material

## Core Idea

The bonus lists group lower-frequency words by the kind of risk they create, which makes them useful as editing filters even when individual terms are not memorized.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Category-based edit pass**
  - When to use: after searching the Top 50.
  - How: scan for categories rather than individual entries: bad adverbs, fancy words, lawyerly words, sentence adverbs, technical-guide words, and words that do not belong in briefs.

- **Sentence-adverb purge**
  - When to use: for sentence openers like actually, apparently, basically, certainly, clearly, evidently, frankly, hopefully, interestingly, ironically, presumably, technically, ultimately, and unfortunately.
  - How: delete the opener and see whether the sentence loses anything but attitude.

- **Legal obligation exception**
  - When to use: for legal words such as adjudicated, adverse, binding, circumstantial, discretionary, interlocutory, negligent, preclusive, quashed, stipulated, verified, void, and vicarious.
  - How: keep them only when they serve a precise legal function.

## Key Concepts

- **Bad adverb**: An adverb that sounds awkward, invented, or unnecessarily decorative.
- **Fancy-word risk**: Vocabulary that shifts attention from the argument to the writer's diction.
- **Lawyerly word**: A term that may be necessary in doctrine but stilted elsewhere.
- **Technical-guide word**: Administrative or procedural diction that makes prose sound mechanical.
- **Brief-inappropriate word**: Casual, cartoonish, insulting, or melodramatic language.

## Mental Models

- Use the bonus lists as **search seeds** when polishing a brief.
- Think of sentence adverbs as **announced attitude**; remove the announcement and make the point.
- Treat long or ornate words as **reader-friction costs** that must buy real precision.
- Do not use a legal word merely because the audience is legal.

## Anti-patterns

- **Sentence adverbs that perform honesty or confidence**: frankly, honestly, truthfully, certainly.
- **Fancy words with uncertain meaning**: abstruse, inchoate, pellucid, propitious, risible, tautological.
- **Instruction-manual prose**: operational, qualitative, quantitative, situational, duplicative.
- **Casual insult language**: daft, janky, loony, pesky, savage, smarmy, venomous.

## Key Takeaways

1. The category matters more than the individual word.
2. If a word sounds like vocabulary performance, replace it.
3. If a word sounds like administrative machinery, simplify it.
4. If a word sounds like irritation, remove it unless the feeling is legally material.
5. Bonus lists are best used as polish passes after substance is complete.
