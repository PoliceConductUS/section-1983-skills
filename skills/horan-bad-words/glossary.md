# Glossary

**Absolute** - A claim that admits no exception, such as always, never,
indisputable, or total. Use only when the record supports it.

**Adjective/adverb audit** - A pass that tests every modifier for necessity,
precision, and tone.

**Bad faith language** - Wording that attributes dishonest motive, hidden
purpose, or insincerity. Use only with support and legal relevance.

**Filler** - A word that adds rhythm or emphasis but not meaning.

**Hedging** - Language that weakens precision while appearing careful, such as
generally, largely, possible, or potentially.

**Intensifier** - A word that increases volume rather than substance: very,
really, wholly, utterly, totally, clearly.

**Legalese** - Lawyerly phrasing that is not required by doctrine and can be
replaced with ordinary English.

**Mind-reading** - Attributing intent, emotion, sincerity, or motive without
record support.

**Overstatement** - A claim whose certainty, severity, or universality exceeds
the support offered.

**Plain-English substitution** - Replacing legalese or ornate diction with a
simpler word that carries the same meaning.

**Rhetorical flourish** - A phrase that calls attention to the writer's style or
attitude instead of advancing analysis.

**Sentence adverb** - A sentence-level opener or modifier that often announces
attitude: frankly, clearly, apparently, interestingly, technically.

**Show, do not tell** - Replace labels and conclusions with facts, evidence,
law, and reasoning.

**Term of art** - A word or phrase required by a governing legal standard,
doctrine, rule, statute, or quotation.

**Throat-clearer** - Introductory verbiage such as "it is important to note" or
"the truth is" that delays the point.

**Tone laundering** - Using elevated vocabulary to make an insult or accusation
sound more professional.

**Top 50** - Horan's highest-priority list of common legal-writing words to
avoid or heavily scrutinize.

**Weasel word** - A vague qualifier that avoids exactness while appearing to add
nuance.
